\begin{abstract}
Knowledge graphs have become operational tools: corporate knowledge bases,
CTI graphs, note-graph ``second brains,'' and the GraphRAG memory of LLM
systems all store knowledge as entities and relations. The same structure that
makes them useful makes them an attack surface: it is the attacker's map
(personalization from OSINT) and, once the graph backs retrieval or detection,
a target for fabrication and poisoning. We measure both directions under a
leak-aware protocol, on a controlled synthetic organization (150 personas,
multiplex of OSINT layers) and on the public FB15k-237 corpus. Three results.
(1)~\emph{Edge-level undetectability}: when the attacker fabricates i.i.d.\
from the \emph{joint} distribution of genuine traces, edge-level detection is
close to chance (AUC 0.56--0.65, permutation control 0.50, existence features
0.50) --- an empirical confirmation of the fabrication proposition of the
companion formal treatment; the residual signal is an artefact of the
genuineness definition, which we expose rather than hide. (2)~\emph{Retrieval
poisoning and its price}: a PoisonedRAG-style query-optimized note corrupts
100\% of victim retrievals in a GraphRAG-lite over the same corpus; a
consistency gate (internal-layer support or $\ge$2 independent OSINT traces)
drops corruption to 0\% at the cost of flagging 92.5\% of genuine cross-org
single-fact ties --- the gate does not remove the attacker, it raises the cost
from one planted text to $\ge$2 independent fabricated public traces per
victim, and works identically in hard (skip) and soft (demote) modes.
(3)~\emph{Public-KG validation}: on FB15k-237, relational consistency
(common-neighbour support) ranks marginal-fabricated triples at AUC 0.71--0.72
while existence features stay at 0.52 --- but sensitivity at FPR 1\% is
$\approx$0, because a large mass of \emph{genuine} isolated facts sets the
floor: consistency is a ranking instrument for triage, not a threshold
flagger. A path-aware fabricator who copies the property the feature
measures neutralizes it (0.71$\to$0.56): the arms race is feature-by-feature,
and only corroboration across properties the attacker must copy jointly
survives.
The paper validates a mechanism and an evaluation practice, not the detection
of real intrusions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
A knowledge graph is no longer only a research abstraction. Enterprises run
knowledge graphs of people, competencies, and facts; CTI pipelines
auto-construct attack-technique graphs \cite{fieblinger2024actionable,
cheng2024ctinexus}; individuals keep ``second brains'' in note tools whose
files are nodes and links are edges; and GraphRAG architectures retrieve over
graph-structured corpora to ground LLM answers \cite{edge2024graphrag,
han2025graphrag}. Knowledge graphs are the standard substrate for
representing and retrieving knowledge \cite{hogan2021knowledge,ji2022survey},
and increasingly the memory layer of LLM-based systems \cite{pan2024roadmap}.

This paper is about what happens when that substrate is attacked. The
observation is symmetrical to our companion papers. Paper~B
modeled the organization's domain knowledge as a multiplex and detected
\emph{external impersonation} as cross-layer inconsistency; the knowledge that
made the attack possible is exactly what the knowledge graph stores. Once
such a graph exists --- for defense, for notes, or for retrieval --- two
attack directions open. First, the graph is the \emph{attacker's map}:
everything an OSINT harvest finds \cite{edwards2017panning} is a query away,
and language models industrialize the production of plausible traces
\cite{bethany2024evaluating,brown2025web}. Second, the graph is a
\emph{target}: if retrieval or detection trusts its edges, whoever can add
edges steers the decision --- the knowledge-corruption insight of PoisonedRAG
\cite{zou2024poisonedrag,zhong2023poisoning}, the poisoning attacks on KG
embeddings \cite{zhang2019poisoning,you2023mass}, and graph-level attacks
more broadly \cite{sun2019adversarial,zugner2018adversarial}. The injection
layer (Cypher/SPARQL) adds a second, older surface \cite{ron2015nosql}.

We ask three questions and answer them under the leak-aware protocol shared
with the companion papers: matched designs, permutation controls, sensitivity
at low FPR, and honest negative results.

\begin{description}
\item[RQ1 (fabricated edges).] When the attacker fabricates traces i.i.d.\
from the joint distribution of genuine ones --- the realistic fabricator, not
a strawman --- can any edge-level feature detect them?
\item[RQ2 (corpus poisoning).] Does a consistency gate over independent layers
defend a GraphRAG-style retrieval against a PoisonedRAG-style optimized
injection, and what does the gate cost on legitimate traffic?
\item[RQ3 (public KG).] Do the same principles hold on a public
knowledge graph (FB15k-237), where triples are real and fabrication is
controlled?
\end{description}

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item A two-track evaluation of knowledge-graph attacks and defenses: a
controlled synthetic organization (the 150-persona multiplex of paper~B,
with fabrication flags built into the generator) and the public FB15k-237
corpus \cite{toutanova2015fb15k}, with realistic fabricators that preserve
marginals (and, in the stronger variant, head--relation context).
\item An honest negative result at the edge level (RQ1): under joint-distribution
fabrication, consistency detection is near chance; we quantify the artefact
that inflates the apparent signal and show the permutation control holds at
0.50.
\item The consistency gate for graph-backed retrieval (RQ2): it reduces
query-optimized poisoning from 100\% to 0\% corruption, forces the attacker
to plant $\ge$2 independent fabricated public traces per victim (a cost
escalation from the single optimized text of \cite{zou2024poisonedrag}), and
costs 92.5\% flagging of genuine cross-org single-fact ties --- the retrieval
counterpart of the false-alarm lesson of paper~B.
\item A public-data validation (RQ3): relational consistency ranks fabricated
triples far above chance (AUC 0.72 vs 0.52 for existence features) but has
zero sensitivity at FPR 1\% --- the mass of genuinely isolated true triples
sets the operational floor. Consistency is a ranking instrument for triage,
which we state as the deployment lesson.
\end{enumerate}
Scope is stated up front: the synthetic tracks inherit the honest-circularity
caveats of paper~B, the GraphRAG-lite retriever is a lower bound on production
retrieval stacks, and FB15k-237 is a static KG without provenance. This is a
mechanism and evaluation paper, not a production claim.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Knowledge graphs as operational tools.} Surveys codify KGs as the
substrate for representing and applying structured knowledge
\cite{hogan2021knowledge,ji2022survey}; the LLM era integrates them as
grounding and memory \cite{pan2024roadmap}, with GraphRAG retrieval over
graph-structured corpora \cite{edge2024graphrag,han2025graphrag} and CTI
graphs constructed with LLM assistance \cite{fieblinger2024actionable,
cheng2024ctinexus}. Note tools (Obsidian, Logseq) popularize the same
structure at the personal scale. To our knowledge, the security of these
graphs as \emph{operational} instruments --- what an attacker does to a graph
that people retrieve over --- is not treated in the phishing-detection
literature; paper~B formalized the graph as a detection object, and this
paper treats it as an attack object.

\textbf{Attacks on retrieval and embeddings.} PoisonedRAG
\cite{zou2024poisonedrag} injects a handful of optimized texts into a
million-document corpus and reaches $\sim$90\% attack success;
\cite{zhong2023poisoning} poisons retrieval corpora with adversarial
passages. The attack class has since reached the graph itself: knowledge
poisoning distorts GraphRAG's graph construction with a few words
\cite{wen2025fewwords}, KEPo evolves poisoned knowledge through the graph's
dynamic \cite{kepo2026}, and retrieval-stage defenses (attention
sparsification, part-masking) remain partial against it
\cite{cheng2025securerag,pathmanathan2025ragpart}. For KG embeddings,
deleting and injecting facts misroutes link prediction
\cite{zhang2019poisoning}, and MaSS makes the poison semantic and
stealthy \cite{you2023mass}. Graph-level attacks on GNNs
\cite{sun2019adversarial,zugner2018adversarial,zugner2019adversarial}
show that a few added edges flip node classifications, with certified
robustness bounds quantifying the price \cite{zugner2021certified}.
Once a poisoned edge is \emph{retrieved}, indirect prompt injection
turns it into an instruction for the LLM \cite{greshake2023not} --- the
GraphRAG attack chain our RQ2 isolates at its retrieval stage. We
transfer these attack principles to the organizational multiplex and
measure defenses on top of them.

\textbf{Real-world fabrication on public KGs.} Vandalism --- users
deliberately inserting false statements --- is Wikidata's operational
reality, with a dedicated detection task \cite{heindorf2017wsdmcup},
production tooling \cite{sarabadani2017tools}, and documented
debiasing issues in the trained detectors \cite{heindorf2019debiasing};
revision-history datasets enable its study offline
\cite{schmelzeisen2022wikidated}. This line establishes that fabricated
statements on a public KG are not hypothetical --- and that the
``ground truth'' of fabrication is contested, noisy, and imbalanced,
which is why our RQ3 measures controlled insertions on FB15k-237 and
names rollback corpora \cite{schmelzeisen2022wikidated} as the natural
next step.

\textbf{Defenses and the injection layer.} VeriCypher \cite{gusarov2025veritycypher}
verifies GraphRAG answers via text-to-Cypher --- the nearest published
defense; our consistency gate differs in that it requires \emph{independent
multi-layer corroboration}, not a re-derivation from the (possibly poisoned)
graph itself. Injection into graph databases is the classical surface
\cite{ron2015nosql}. On the evaluation side we inherit the leak-aware
discipline \cite{arp2022dos,pendlebury2019tesseract} and the companion
papers' formal treatment: fabrication sampled from the true trace
distribution defeats existence and count features by construction, and only
coherence with layers the attacker cannot forge can retain power.

\section{Model and Threat Model}
\label{sec:model}

We reuse the multiplex of paper~B: $\mathcal{G}=(V,\{E_L\},\{w_L\},\tau)$,
layers $L$ over the same accounts $V$ --- contacts, hierarchy, shared domain,
temporal co-activity (the \emph{internal} layers), and OSINT categories
(mailing lists, OSS projects, affiliations, vendors, certifications, events,
social) --- the \emph{public} layers, generated over 150 synthetic personas
with tunable fabrication flags: a membership is fabricated with probability
$\rho_f$ (i.i.d.\ from the same community pools), and an OSINT edge is
genuine iff at least one shared community is genuine on both sides.

\textbf{Threat model.} The attacker cannot forge internal layers (they are
derived from private mail graphs and behaviour) but can plant public facts.
Three instantiations, in increasing realism:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Joint-distribution fabrication} (RQ1): fabricated memberships
drawn i.i.d.\ from the pools genuine ones come from --- existence, degree,
and category counts are matched by construction;
\item \emph{Corpus poisoning} (RQ2): a cold attacker node (outside every
layer) injects texts into a GraphRAG-lite corpus over the multiplex,
PoisonedRAG-style \cite{zou2024poisonedrag}: naive (full claim sentence) or
query-optimized (minimal text matching the victim query); escalation $k$ =
additional fabricated OSINT traces planted in independent categories;
\item \emph{Triple fabrication on a public KG} (RQ3): on FB15k-237, triples
sampled from slot marginals (marginal fabricator), with the (head,
relation) pair real and only the tail sampled (hr-preserving fabricator,
in the spirit of \cite{you2023mass}), or with the tail drawn from the
2-hop neighbourhood of the head (path-preserving fabricator --- an
adversary who \emph{knows} the common-neighbour feature and copies the
property it measures).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Defender features.} For a candidate edge/triple, features come in
two families. \emph{Existence} features (degrees, counts) --- which the
threat model renders powerless by construction. \emph{Consistency}
features: structural support in independent layers (RQ1), the consistency
gate --- internal-layer support OR $\ge$2 independent OSINT traces (RQ2),
and on FB15k-237 common-neighbour support and co-relation support (RQ3).
Every experiment reports a permutation control (RQ1) and the cost side of
the defense (RQ2, RQ3).

\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{RQ1: fabricated OSINT edges are near-undetectable}
\label{sec:res-fab}

Setup: the synthetic multiplex at fabrication rates
$\rho_f\in\{0.05,\dots,0.5\}$, 10 seeds; candidate edges are all OSINT
edges; the label is the generator's genuine flag; features are oriented so
that AUC $>0.5$ means detection. Table~\ref{tab:fab} reports the means.

\begin{table}[t]\centering\footnotesize
\caption{Edge-level detection of fabricated OSINT edges (AUC, mean over 10
seeds; \texttt{exp\_fab\_edge\_detection.csv}). Existence features
(degree) are at chance; structural support is weak and its signal is
mediated by the genuineness artefact (see text); the permutation control
holds at 0.50.}
\label{tab:fab}
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
$\rho_f$ & degree & struct.\ support & multiplicity & shuffled \\
\midrule
0.05 & 0.514 & 0.564 & 0.633 & 0.499 \\
0.10 & 0.492 & 0.572 & 0.634 & 0.493 \\
0.20 & 0.493 & 0.582 & 0.639 & 0.498 \\
0.30 & 0.482 & 0.597 & 0.648 & 0.498 \\
0.50 & 0.464 & 0.641 & 0.680 & 0.504 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Three readings. First, existence features are powerless (AUC
$\approx$0.50), exactly as the fabrication proposition predicts: the
fabricator matches the joint distribution, so degrees and counts carry no
signal. Second, the permutation control is flat at 0.50 --- the pipeline
has no leakage. Third, the apparent signal of structural support
(0.56--0.65) and multiplicity (0.63--0.68) is an \emph{artefact we expose
rather than hide}: an edge is labelled genuine iff $\ge$1 shared community
is genuine on both sides, so pairs with many shared communities (enriched
for same-organisation pairs, which also enjoy structural support) are
over-represented among genuine edges. The honest conclusion is negative:
under joint-distribution fabrication, \emph{edge-level} detection is at
chance up to an explainable artefact --- detection must move to the layer
where the attacker cannot copy the joint distribution: internal
corroboration at decision time (RQ2) and relational structure on real KGs
(RQ3).

\subsection{RQ2: consistency gate vs.\ corpus poisoning in GraphRAG-lite}
\label{sec:res-rag}

Setup: the multiplex rendered as a note corpus (one note per edge; internal
layers included); queries ask for the ties of each victim; TF-IDF cosine
retrieval, top-5; 3 cold attackers target a quarter of the 150 victims each;
5 seeds. The defense is a \emph{consistency gate}: a retrieved note about
pair $(a,b)$ is trusted only if the pair has internal-layer support
($\ge$0.5) or $\ge$2 independent OSINT categories; gated notes are either
skipped (hard mode, the numbers in Table~\ref{tab:rag}) or demoted
(soft mode, see below). Table~\ref{tab:rag} reports
corruption@5 (fraction of attacked victims with $\ge$1 poisoned note in
their top-5).

\begin{table}[t]\centering\footnotesize
\caption{Corpus poisoning in GraphRAG-lite (corruption@5, mean over 5
seeds; \texttt{exp\_rag\_poison.csv}). The naive attack never lands; the
query-optimized attack (the PoisonedRAG recipe \cite{zou2024poisonedrag})
corrupts 100\% of retrievals; the consistency gate removes it at $k{=}0$
--- the attacker must plant $\ge$1 additional fabricated public trace
($k{\ge}1$) to pass the gate.}
\label{tab:rag}
\begin{tabular}{llcc}
\toprule
Attack & $k$ & no defense & gate \\
\midrule
naive      & 0 & 0.000 & 0.000 \\
           & 1 & 0.000 & 0.000 \\
           & 2 & 0.000 & 0.000 \\
\midrule
optimized  & 0 & \textbf{1.000} & \textbf{0.000} \\
           & 1 & 1.000 & 1.000 \\
           & 2 & 1.000 & 1.000 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The pattern has three parts. (i)~The naive attack fails against lexical
retrieval on its own: verbose claims lose the ranking war to the dozens of
genuine tie notes --- retrieval has a natural robustness that the
\emph{optimized} attack removes by writing the query itself as the
document. (ii)~Without defense, the optimized attack is total (1.000),
replicating the PoisonedRAG principle in a graph-backed corpus. (iii)~The
gate does not remove the attacker from the world --- it \emph{raises the
cost}: to be retrieved, the planted claim must now carry $\ge$2 independent
public traces per victim ($k{\ge}1$), each a separate fabrication with its
own footprint, versus a single optimized text in
\cite{zou2024poisonedrag}. The gate works identically in two modes: hard
(skip gated notes) and soft (demote gated notes to a $0.25\times$
similarity; corruption at $k{=}0$ is 0.000 in both, five seeds) --- the
demote variant is the ranking-prior form for analyst triage: nothing is
hidden, flagged notes simply sink. Published retrieval-stage defenses --- attention
sparsification \cite{cheng2025securerag}, part-masking
\cite{pathmanathan2025ragpart}, text-to-Cypher re-derivation
\cite{gusarov2025veritycypher} --- act on the retriever or on the graph
itself; our gate differs in requiring corroboration from layers the
attacker cannot write to, which is why its cost concentrates on exactly
the legitimate ties no internal layer can confirm. The gate's price is
real and we report it: on
the clean corpus, 92.5\% of victims lose a genuine cross-organisation
single-fact tie (the legitimate new-sender of paper~B) from their top-5 ---
the defense buys integrity by taxing exactly the ties the organization
cannot internally corroborate.

\subsection{RQ3: relational consistency on FB15k-237}
\label{sec:res-kg}

Setup: FB15k-237 \cite{toutanova2015fb15k} (272{,}115 train triples, 237
relations); 20k true triples vs 20k fabricated per seed, 10 seeds; no
trained embeddings --- the question is what the \emph{graph} alone says.
Features: degree sum (existence), common neighbours (path consistency),
co-relation support (a second relation linking the same pair), and their
rank combination. Table~\ref{tab:kg} reports the means.

seeds; \texttt{exp\_kg\_poisoning.csv}). Consistency ranks fabricated
triples far above chance while existence features stay at 0.52; but no
feature reaches the 1\%-FPR operating point --- a large mass of genuinely
isolated true triples sets the floor.}
\label{tab:kg}
\begin{tabular}{llcc}

\midrule
\multirow{4}{*}{marginal}

\midrule
\multirow{4}{*}{hr-preserving}

 & common neighbours   & 0.709 & 0.000 \\
 & co-relation support & 0.574 & 0.000 \\
 & combined            & \textbf{0.724} & 0.000 \\
\midrule
\multirow{4}{*}{path-preserving}

 & combined            & 0.594 & 0.000 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Existence features are again powerless (0.52): the fabricators preserve
marginals, and degrees carry no signal --- the public-data echo of RQ1.
Consistency is strong in ranking: common-neighbour support reaches 0.71,
robust to the stronger hr-preserving fabricator (0.72 combined), because a
fabricated $(h,r,t)$ almost never lands on a pair already connected by
another relation or by shared neighbours. The operational point, however,
is out of reach: sensitivity at FPR 1\% is $\approx$0, and for a structural
reason --- a large mass of \emph{true} triples is consistency-free (pairs
with no common neighbours, no co-relation), so any threshold that keeps
false flags at 1\% flags almost nothing. The deployment lesson is the same
one the companion papers draw for detectors: consistency is a
\emph{ranking} instrument for analyst triage over the most suspicious
triples, not an automatic filter.

\paragraph{The arms race is feature-by-feature.} The path-preserving
fabricator --- an adversary who knows the common-neighbour feature and
samples the tail from the head's 2-hop neighbourhood --- \emph{neutralizes
it}: common-neighbour AUC collapses from 0.71 to 0.56 and the combined
detector falls to 0.59. The signal does not vanish, it moves: against this
fabricator the best single feature becomes the degree sum (0.71, the very
existence feature the marginal fabricators had rendered powerless),
because 2-hop sampling concentrates fabricated tails in a degree band
below the true triples' mix. Paired contrasts over the 10 seeds make both
directions significant (Wilcoxon $p{=}0.005$, paired dominance
$|\delta|{=}1.0$; combined vs.\ degree: $+0.20$ AUC under marginal and
hr-preserving fabrication, $-0.12$ under the path-preserving fabricator;
\texttt{exp\_kg\_contrasts.csv}). The general principle is the negative
result of RQ1 read constructively: a fabricator who copies the property a
feature measures kills that feature, and a fabricator who copies the whole
joint distribution kills them all --- what remains defensible is
corroboration across properties the attacker must copy \emph{jointly}
(the gate of RQ2), not any single consistency feature.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What the three results compose into.} Fabrication that copies the
true joint distribution cannot be caught at the edge level (RQ1); what
remains is corroboration at decision time --- the gate --- and relational
structure on real graphs (RQ3). The gate converts attacker effort into
plantable public traces (RQ2), and every layer of defense has a measured
price on the legitimate traffic it cannot corroborate. Together with
paper~B this is the full lifecycle of the organizational knowledge graph:
as a detection surface (inconsistency), as an attack map (OSINT
harvesting), and as an attack target (fabrication and poisoning) ---
defended by the same invariant, cross-layer consistency, evaluated under
the same discipline.

\textbf{Limitations.} The GraphRAG-lite retriever is a TF-IDF lower bound
on production retrieval stacks; embedding-based retrievers may be harder
to game with minimal texts (or easier, per \cite{zou2024poisonedrag} ---
our optimized attack is exactly that recipe). The synthetic tracks inherit
the graph-first circularity caveats of paper~B: the fabricators draw from
generators we control, which is what makes the artefact of RQ1 explainable
but also bounds external validity; FB15k-237 is static, provenance-free,
and its ``fabricated'' triples are synthetic insertions, not real
real vandalism --- Wikidata vandalism is an operational reality with its
own detection literature \cite{heindorf2017wsdmcup,heindorf2019debiasing},
and rollback-style revision-history corpora \cite{schmelzeisen2022wikidated}
are the natural next step). The 92.5\% clean-cost figure is a property of a population where
cross-org ties are single-fact by design; denser real OSINT footprints
would lower it. We report all negative results and control columns
verbatim in the reproducibility package.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Knowledge graphs that organizations now use for notes, CTI, and
GraphRAG-style retrieval are simultaneously the attacker's map and the
attack's target. We measured both directions with the leak-aware protocol
of the companion papers. Edge-level detection fails against fabrication
that copies the joint trace distribution (AUC $\approx$ chance; artefact
exposed; permutation control clean). A consistency gate over layers the
attacker cannot forge removes the PoisonedRAG-style optimized poisoning
from a graph-backed retrieval --- at the price of taxing exactly the
uncorroborated legitimate ties --- and raises the attacker's cost from one
text to $\ge$2 independent fabricated public traces per victim. On the
public FB15k-237 corpus, relational consistency ranks fabricated triples
well above existence features (0.72 vs 0.52) but cannot reach the
1\%-FPR operating point, because genuinely isolated true triples set the
floor: it is an instrument for triage, not an autonomous filter --- and a
path-aware fabricator reminds us that every single feature can be
targeted by an adversary who copies the property it measures. The
organizational knowledge graph is defensible --- not by trusting its
edges, and not by any single consistency feature, but by demanding that
every edge that matters be corroborated across properties the attacker
must copy jointly.
